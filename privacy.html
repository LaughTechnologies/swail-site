Swail

Privacy policy

Last updated: 16 November 2026

In short. Swail is an app that helps you clear out your email. It reads your mailbox on your phone and keeps what it learns on your phone. There is no Swail server and no Swail account, so your email, your passwords and your choices are never sent to us. Swail has no ads, no analytics and no tracking.

Who is responsible

Swail is made by Laugh Technologies Ltd (“we”, “us”). For any question about this policy or your privacy, contact us at CONTACT-TO-FILL.

What Swail reads

Swail reads only the inboxes you connect, and only to show you who emails you and let you choose what to clear out. What it reads depends on how the inbox is connected.

Gmail

You sign in on Google’s own sign-in screen and allow Swail to read and organise your Gmail. Swail then talks to Gmail directly from your phone. It reads:

  • your Google account’s name, email address and account ID, to show which inbox is connected;
  • for each email you have received (archived email included; not Spam, Trash, drafts or chats): who it is from, who it was sent to (To and Cc), its subject, its date, its size, the first lines of its text as Gmail summarises them, whether it is unread or starred, whether it has an attachment, whether Gmail files it under Promotions, and the unsubscribe link the sender put in it, if there is one;
  • which conversations you have written in, so that Swail can protect them;
  • the full text of an email and the names, types and sizes of its attachments, only when you open that email in Swail.

The permission Google asks you to grant is described by Google more broadly than this (it also covers writing and sending email). Swail does not write, send or permanently delete email.

Outlook (Outlook.com, Hotmail, Live, Microsoft 365)

You sign in on Microsoft’s own sign-in page. Swail then talks to Microsoft’s mail service (Microsoft Graph) directly from your phone. It reads your Microsoft account’s name, email address and account ID, and the same kind of information as for Gmail from your Inbox and Sent Items: sender, recipients, subject, date, size, the first lines, whether an email is read or flagged, whether it has an attachment, its unsubscribe link, and the full text and attachment names of an email only when you open it.

iCloud, Yahoo, AOL and other email (IMAP)

You give Swail your email address and a password (for many providers, an app password you create for Swail). Swail connects from your phone straight to your mail provider’s server over an encrypted connection and signs in with them. It reads, from your Inbox and your Sent folder: sender, recipients, subject, date, size, whether an email is read or flagged, its unsubscribe link, and the content of an email only when you open it.

If Swail does not already know your provider’s mail server, it looks it up in Mozilla’s public list of mail settings. That request contains only the part of your address after the “@” (for example “example.com”), never the address itself.

Where your data is kept

On your phone, and nowhere else. Swail has no server. Nothing Swail reads from your mailbox is sent to us or to anyone else.

  • What is saved. The results of the scan (for each email: sender, recipients, subject, date, size, first lines, and whether it is unread, starred or has an attachment), the list of senders with their unsubscribe links, and the choices you make. They are saved in an encrypted database on your phone. Its key is kept in the phone’s secure storage (the iPhone Keychain or the Android Keystore), can be read only while the phone is unlocked, and is never copied to another device.
  • What is not saved. The full text of an email you open is shown to you and not saved.
  • Android backups. On Android, Swail’s data is left out of the phone’s backups.

The sign-ins Swail keeps

To keep working without asking you to sign in every time, Swail keeps a sign-in for each connected inbox on your phone:

  • Gmail on iPhone: a token from Google that lets Swail ask for access again, kept in the iPhone Keychain.
  • Gmail on Android: your permission is held by Google Play services on your phone, for each account; Swail itself stores no Google token.
  • Outlook: a token from Microsoft, kept in the iPhone Keychain or in Android’s secure storage.
  • Other email (IMAP): your mail server’s address, your user name and the password you entered, kept in the iPhone Keychain or in Android’s secure storage. The password is sent only to your own mail provider’s server, to sign in.

Everything Swail keeps this way can be read only while the phone is unlocked and is never copied to another device. We never see your passwords or tokens.

What Swail changes in your mailbox

Only what you confirm. When you confirm a clean-up, Swail moves the emails you chose to your mailbox’s Trash (“Deleted Items” in Outlook). It does not delete them permanently: your mail provider keeps them in Trash for a time (Gmail: 30 days) and you can take them back from there.

Unsubscribe requests

When you turn on “Stop future emails” for a sender, Swail uses the one-click unsubscribe link that sender put in its own emails. Your phone sends one request to that link, over an encrypted connection. The request carries no cookies and nothing about you beyond what the sender put in its own link; like any web request, it shows the sender your phone’s internet (IP) address. Swail only does this for senders whose emails offer a one-click link.

Who else your phone talks to

Besides your mail provider and the senders you unsubscribe from, Swail contacts:

  • The update check. When it opens, Swail reads a small public file (release.json) from this website, which is hosted by GitHub, to learn whether a newer version exists or the version you have is too old to use. It also asks the app update service run by Expo whether an update is ready, and downloads it if so. These requests contain nothing from your mailbox and do not say who you are; like any internet request they show your IP address and technical details about the app (such as its version and whether it is the iPhone or Android app) to those services.
  • Web pages you choose to open from Swail, such as your provider’s page for creating an app password or the store page for a new version, open in your browser.

Swail contains no advertising, no analytics and no crash-reporting service, and it does not sell or share your data with anyone.

The store you got Swail from (the App Store, TestFlight or Google Play) is run by Apple or Google under their own privacy policies.

Google user data: Limited Use

Swail’s use of information received from Google APIs (Gmail) will adhere to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements. In particular:

  • Swail uses Gmail data only to provide the features you see in the app: showing who emails you, moving the emails you choose to Trash, and unsubscribing you from senders you choose.
  • Gmail data is not transferred to us or to anyone else. It stays on your phone.
  • Gmail data is not used for advertising.
  • No person at Laugh Technologies Ltd can read your Gmail data, because we never receive it.
  • Gmail data is not used to develop, improve or train artificial intelligence or machine learning models.

Taking access back and deleting your data

  • Disconnect an inbox in Swail (open the inbox’s page and choose “Disconnect this inbox”). This erases everything Swail saved about that inbox and deletes its sign-in from your phone. Disconnecting your last inbox erases all of Swail’s saved data.
  • Gmail: disconnecting also tells Google to withdraw Swail’s access (if Google cannot be reached, Swail tries again the next time it opens). You can also remove Swail yourself at any time in your Google Account, under third-party apps and services.
  • Outlook: disconnecting deletes the sign-in from your phone. Microsoft does not let an app withdraw its own permission, so to remove it at Microsoft too, go to your Microsoft account → Privacy → Apps and services, and remove Swail.
  • Other email: disconnecting deletes the saved password from your phone. If you created an app password for Swail, you can also delete it at your mail provider.
  • Deleting the app: disconnect your inboxes first, then delete Swail. This makes sure the saved sign-ins are removed from your phone as well.

Because your data never reaches us, we hold nothing about you that we could show you, correct or delete: it is all on your phone, under your control. If you have a question or a request about your privacy, write to us at CONTACT-TO-FILL and we will answer.
